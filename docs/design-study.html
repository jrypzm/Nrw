Years · Design study 2 · Android

Your years, on the screen you look at most

The web app proved the core idea: one number and one grid of dots. This study takes it to Android, onto the home screen as widgets and onto the lock screen as a wallpaper that changes on its own, slowly, as your life moves.

Example used throughout: age 34, 80 year lifespan, 45 years left. Each dot is a year.
What the research says

Seven findings that shape the design

A reminder fades unless you see it daily

Reviews of memento mori apps found static counters impress once, then get ignored. The ones people keep put the reminder on the lock screen or pair it with a tiny daily habit.

The lock screen is a wallpaper problem

Samsung One UI only allows its own widgets on the lock screen. Android 16 added lock screen widgets in a separate "hub" page on Pixels only. A wallpaper is the one route that reaches every phone.

Competitors update once a day

Life Calendar, DotLife and similar apps redraw at midnight and stop there. None of them makes the picture feel alive. That gap is the opportunity.

Widgets can't tick every second

Android refreshes a widget at most every 30 minutes on its own schedule, or every 15 with WorkManager. A widget should show calm, slowly changing facts, not a live counter.

A live wallpaper must sleep

Android's own guidance: draw only while the wallpaper is visible. Done that way, a slow animation costs almost nothing. Done badly, it drains the battery and gets uninstalled.

Trust is a feature

The best rated apps in this space say clearly: no account, no data leaves the phone, and the lifespan number is explained. Users check this.

Premium means restraint

Design guides agree that the premium feel comes from consistent detail: one corner radius, slower spring motion, a light vibration on each touch, numbers in tabular figures. Not from more features.

Principles

Five rules every screen follows

One number, one grid

Every surface shows at most one number and one dot pattern. If a design needs a third element, it's the wrong design.

Black is the canvas

True black, white dots and two greys. No colour, no gradients on the content. On an OLED screen, black pixels are off, so the dots seem to float.

Motion is time

Things only move when time actually passes: the current year's dot fills, the light shifts through the day. Nothing moves for decoration.

Quiet until touched

Labels, ages and dates appear only when you touch a dot. The resting state is just dots.

Private by default

No account, no network, no analytics. Your birthday never leaves the phone.

Home screen widgets

Four widgets, each with one job

All four come in three backgrounds the user picks when adding the widget: Black (melts into a dark wallpaper), Surface (soft grey card) and Glass (faint frosted edge). They refresh at midnight and on your birthday, which is all a year-based widget needs.

Lock screen

A wallpaper that changes on its own, slowly

The lock screen is seen around 80 times a day. Because Samsung won't take third-party lock screen widgets, the app owns the lock screen through the wallpaper. Five directions, all running live below. The motion is sped up here so you can see it; on the phone it's much slower.

How "changes automatically" works on Android. The static Quiet grid is a picture the app swaps every midnight with WorkManager. The live versions (Daylight, Unlock ripple, Hourglass, Constellation) are a real live wallpaper: Android runs our drawing code only while the lock or home screen is visible, and the app redraws a frame every few minutes, or for one second after you wake the screen. Samsung applies live wallpapers to home and lock screen together, so those designs need to look good behind app icons too.

Features

What to build, in order

Grouped by how much each one adds compared with what it costs to build. Everything stays years-only, as agreed.

Version 1 · must have

Home screen widgets

Life (4×2) and Number (2×2), with Black, Surface and Glass backgrounds. Previews in the widget picker show your real dots.

Lock screen wallpaper

Quiet grid (static, midnight refresh) plus Daylight (live). The wallpaper picker inside the app shows a live preview with your clock position.

Three number styles

Classic, Large and Live from the web app, now with SF Pro bundled in the app.

Drag through your life

The scrub gesture with a haptic tick per year, already in the web app, using Android's precise vibration effects.

Lifespan you can trust

Default 80, or an estimate from country and sex using offline UN life expectancy tables, with a line explaining where the number comes from.

Version 1.1 · should have

Milestones

Long-press a dot to name a year: "Moved to Dubai", "Started the company". Named years get a thin ring. The past becomes a story, not just a count.

Birthday moment

On your birthday the app opens to a slow animation: last year's dot fills completely, then the next one starts. Optional quiet notification that morning.

Decade and This year widgets

The two zoomed-in widgets for people who find 80 dots too abstract.

Share card

Export a clean square image of your grid, for a status or story. No branding beyond a tiny wordmark.

Later · could have

Hourglass and Constellation

The two bolder live wallpapers, once the core is solid.

One question a week

An optional Sunday prompt such as "What did this week add to your year?" The research says a small habit keeps the reminder from fading. Answers stay on the phone.

Pixel lock screen hub

Android 16 lock screen widgets on Pixel phones. Small audience, so it waits.

Wear OS tile

A ten-dot decade tile for the watch.

How it's built

Native Android, Kotlin

A web app can't make widgets or wallpapers, so the Android version is native. Kotlin with Jetpack Compose gives the smoothest motion and full access to widgets, wallpapers and vibration. Cross-platform tools (Flutter, React Native) still need native Kotlin code for widgets and wallpapers, so they save little here.
